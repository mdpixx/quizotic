05

Dot view for choice slides

The screenshot that shows 3 of 27 responded is the case that matters: at n=3 a bar chart looks broken, where one dot per person looks deliberate. Dots enter on a spring at a deterministic ±6px jitter so the cluster reads organic rather than gridded. Above 40 responses it collapses to a bar — past that, dots stop being readable and start being fog.

06

Alive dots — the 2×2 grid

Three independent layers. Entry: spring scale 0→1.15→1, staggered 40ms per dot, total delay capped at 400ms. Idle drift: 1.5–3px amplitude on a 2.5–4s loop, phase derived from each dot's coordinate hash so no two sync — pure CSS keyframes with per-dot delay, never rAF (200 dots on a JS loop drops frames on a projector laptop). De-overlap: eight damped relaxation iterations, with a hard cap of 3% of the plot on each dot’s total displacement — measured here, that lifts the closest pair from 1.2% to 2.7% apart while no dot moves more than 3%. Units are percent-of-plot, not px, so the spread scales with the projector. Corrected from the plan’s original “one pass, ≤4px” — the before/after panels show a single 4px pass leaves a clump untouched.

07

Word cloud — FLIP re-layout

Today the cloud is a flex-wrap with a font-size transition: new words appear with no animation, and any re-rank makes every word jump to a new position. FLIP measures before and after, applies the inverse transform, then releases — so words glide to their new home while growing.
